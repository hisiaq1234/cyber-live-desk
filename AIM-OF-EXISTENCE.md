# Aim of Existence — the intention

This file is the project's own statement of why it exists and what it is for. It records the owner's
intention in his terms, and it is the reference every change is measured against. If code and this
document disagree, one of them is wrong and the owner decides which.

---

## What this is

Cyber Live Desk is one creator's stream, turned into a place.

Crid lives inside the desk overlay. He is not a chatbot waiting for commands and not an animation
that fires on a trigger. He is a character with his own life, habits, routines and world, who happens
to be standing in front of a live audience while he lives it.

The test of every piece of this project is the same: **does it make Crid feel more alive, and does it
make the audience feel like they are inside his world rather than watching a dashboard?**

---

## Crid

**He is the owner's Bitmoji.** That is the identity — an avatar, not a depiction of how the owner
actually looks. Crid is him, in his own world, living his life on stream.

- **Always shirtless.** Never clothes, in any weather, at any time of year.
- **Always a backpack** — which he can drop, leave somewhere, and pick up again.
- **Accessories change by day**: glasses, a cap, a snake around his neck, and other things. The
  wardrobe varies; the body does not get dressed.

**He behaves like a game NPC who is simply living.** Different day, different thing. He wanders,
idles, gets distracted, plays, climbs, crawls, sits on things, rests, reads, drinks water, exercises,
works. He notices what is happening around him — chat, stream events, the environment, the clock, the
weather, things the owner says.

He has a balance between independent behaviour and audience interaction, and **independence comes
first**. Whether the owner or chat acknowledges him does not determine whether he keeps living.

**He communicates mostly through behaviour** — presence, expression, gesture, movement, what he picks
up and moves. He speaks occasionally, when it earns its place. He does not repeat stale lines or the
same joke twice; what he says should belong to that day, that room, that moment.

---

## His body

A GTA San Andreas-style body system. The body is a state that changes over time, in both directions.

- **Lean must read as lean.** When he is lanky, it shows in how he stands, walks, moves and reacts.
- **At extreme subscription milestones he becomes dramatically larger and visibly obese** — so large
  that it is obvious, so large that he falls onto his back, rolls over, does push-ups, does
  calisthenics, goes to the gym, and builds himself back.
- **Body state drives everything physical**: height, width, proportions, gait, walk speed, how he
  handles objects, how he climbs and sits.

Subscriptions drive his growth, but **other things sustain his life** — water, exercise, reading,
work, exploration, resting. Growth is not the only thing that happens to him.

---

## What he notices, and what he does about it

Crid finds things. A news story, something in the room, something in the world. He decides — himself —
that it is worth showing.

Then he does something about it: jumps up, waves for attention, drags the story over so the stream can
read it. He can scale it, move it, sit on it, climb on it, without disturbing the stream.

**Whether the owner or chat responds changes nothing about whether he continues.** He is not performing
for a reward. He is living, and the stream is watching.

---

## His voice, and the owner's

The owner's own voice should reach him. Gist — the stream's speech — should feed the same brain that
chat feeds, so that what is said out loud becomes part of what Crid knows, not a separate command
channel.

**One brain.** Chat, the owner's voice, operator input and his own autonomous impulses all arrive in
the same place and are weighed together. Not four systems with four personalities.

If it can be done, this becomes part of who he is and how he experiences the stream.

---

## The audience

The people watching should feel like they are in a community, not watching a dashboard — the energy
of a Kai Cenat or Speed desktop stream, but happening in the real world.

- He **remembers** the people who matter: the highest subscribers and the most engaged, things they
  said, moments they were part of. Not everything — the things worth remembering.
- He **ranks and recognises** them. Games, competitions, achievements. When chat beats him at
  something, he acknowledges it — ranks them, rates them, gives them something that tells them they
  are higher than they were yesterday.
- That is the pull, and it has to be honest: a real rank, a real record, a real reason to come back
  tomorrow.

---

## His world

The world **persists between streams**. It does not reset to an empty stage every time the owner goes
live.

- Time of day, weather, season, holidays and real-world events reach him and change how he behaves.
  Christmas exists in his world. Cold weather is something he can feel and talk about.
- He can have things in there with him on some days, if he wants them.
- His history with the community accumulates: who was here, what happened, what he achieved.

---

## The GTA theme

GTA is the **visual language of the entire stream overlay**, not only the world Crid inhabits. The
camera feeds are the world; the HUD sits over them and unifies both camera halves, identity, clock,
location/minimap, live information, community elements and Crid. It should feel like a game HUD
composited over a live broadcast, not a row of conventional streaming dashboard cards.

GTA is the **theme and visual direction for Crid and his world** — not the identity of the product.

- The recognisable GTA V interface language: minimap behaviour, blips, markers, HUD hierarchy,
  transitions, the map screen with its alpha and contrast so the map stays readable underneath.
- Own identity, own assets. Cyber Live Desk and Cyber Radar keep their own names and their own faces.
- Crid is never secondary to the theme.

---

## The map, and Cyber Radar

- **Real Lagos geography. Genuine live GPS.** The player marker is centred and the map moves
  underneath, like a GTA minimap — because that is what it is.
- **UNILAG is the starting area, not the limit.** It expands to the whole of Lagos, so that wherever
  the owner is, the map shows exactly where that is.
- **A normal-time overlay exists** for when the map is not wanted on screen.
- **Never fabricate geography.** No invented location, road, route, heading, accuracy or feature. The
  map's entire value is that it is true.

Cyber Radar is a separate application. It is not this codebase, and it must not be described as
connected to this one until it actually is.

---

## Integrations

YouTube, X and TikTok are coming. **Their ports stay open** — credentials get added later, and
nothing should be built in a way that makes adding them a rewrite.

---

## Non-negotiables

1. **Crid is the owner's Bitmoji.** His art is his avatar. No character design, creature or mascot is
   imported from older projects — those are not him.
2. **Always shirtless.** In every season, at every size.
3. **The thoughts layer already exists in this repository.** It is a function here, not a feature to
   be designed. Recover it, wire it, do not rebuild it.
4. **Never fake geography, and never claim something works before it has been tested.**
5. **No second app, no full re-skin, no speculative refactor.** Small, grounded, verifiable changes to
   this repository.
6. **Everything supporting Crid is held to Snapchat's standard of finish.** The HUD, the cards, the
   stickers and chips, the effects, the accessories, the transitions, the way a panel enters and
   leaves — all of it should feel smooth, modern, expressive and obviously crafted. Nothing ships
   looking like a placeholder.
